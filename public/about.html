ABOUT TEREN

A place to look closely

Understand how DeFi works, test an assumption, and see what changes.

Why this exists

Providing liquidity changes the tokens you own. Borrowing adds a debt beside your assets. Prices, income and costs affect both. Teren gives you a place to explore these mechanics before drawing a conclusion.

It began with liquidity pools and now includes supplying and borrowing. You can follow a lesson or start with a question of your own. Lessons link to Experiment in a separate tab so you can explore the idea while keeping your place.

Two topics to explore

  • Liquidity: compare providing liquidity with holding, explore price ranges and fee assumptions, and try virtual swaps. Open liquidity experiments or learn about liquidity.
  • Lending: supply ETH, borrow USDC, and see how price and interest change a loan. Compare repayment with adding collateral, explore a borrowing loop and see what an assumed liquidation or unwind would change. Open the lending lab or learn about lending.

Choose your starting point

  • Learn offers short lessons in both topics, with predictions, explanations and follow-up situations. Hints are part of learning; you do not need to calculate an answer.
  • Experiment lets you change a setup and compare outcomes. Prices, fee income, interest rates and collateral rules are assumptions you supply.
  • Inspect contains recorded liquidity evidence: Ethereum WETH/USDC snapshots from Uniswap v2 pools and v3 positions, plus two fixed-position historical cases checked at capture. Lending results are hypothetical; Inspect has no lending records yet.

Start from the beginning or open the topic you need. Every example is accessible without completing a course.

The name

Teren takes its name from τέρεν, the Pontic Greek word for “look.” It reflects the founder’s Pontic roots and a simple approach to understanding DeFi: look closely, test your assumptions, and examine what happens.

The wordmark keeps the name at the centre. Its companion symbol is an open, stylized t.

Privacy and live data

The calculators and bundled records run locally in your browser. Fetching a live pool or position contacts PublicNode, with dRPC as a fallback. The provider receives the requested position ID and other public chain queries, and can see your network IP address. Requests omit credentials and the page referrer. Opening a recorded example or saved file makes no RPC request.

How it is made

Teren is an open-source project maintained by Apostolos Provatidis ↗. It runs in your browser, with read-only Ethereum data and no wallet connection. Saved experiments and learning progress stay in this browser unless you export or share them.

Read the methods for the differences between hypothetical models, recorded snapshots and verified historical accounting. The source code ↗ is available under the MIT license.
