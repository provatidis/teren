How this model works

This Uniswap v2-style full-range model starts with 50% ETH and 50% USDC by dollar value. USDC stays at $1. Ideal, frictionless arbitrage brings the pool price to the future market price.

For an initial investment V and price ratio r = future price / starting price, holding is worth V × (1 + r) / 2. The LP assets are worth V × √r before fee income. Initial ETH = V / (2 × starting price); after rebalancing, LP ETH = initial ETH / √r and LP USDC = initial USDC × √r.

Fee income is either a total cash assumption or starting capital × assumed average fee APR × days / 365. It is added as separate USD cash at the end. Fee income and costs are held fixed across the comparison; fee income does not compound, and is not inferred from market prices or trading volume. Assumed LP costs are subtracted as separate USD expenses. Incentives, depegging and concentrated liquidity are excluded. It shows endpoints, not a trading path.

How this range model works

Prices are USD per ETH, with USDC fixed at $1. The starting range determines your token mix; concentrated positions do not always begin 50/50. Holding keeps those exact starting tokens. The full-range LP invests the same amount in a separate 50/50 position.

For liquidity L and lower/upper prices a and b, clamp the current price p to the range. The position holds ETH = L × (1/√p − 1/√b) and USDC = L × (√p − √a). L is sized so those assets equal your investment at the starting price. Below a the position is all ETH; above b it is all USDC.

This simplified Uniswap v3-style model allows any valid price bounds; real v3 positions use tick-aligned bounds. It does not apply Uniswap tick spacing or on-chain rounding, and does not forecast fees or trading paths. Range fee income is either a cash amount or starting capital × assumed average fee APR × days / 365, including any time out of range. Fees remain separate USD cash without compounding. Opening, collection and closing costs are extra USD expenses compared with holding. The optional 50/50 reference uses its own cash fees and the same cost budget. Incentives are excluded.

        Go deeper: arbitrage and changing fee competition

        Why do pools trade when the market moves?

        Follow one price change. This separate illustration does not add fees or trades to your experiment.

        A full-range, zero-fee illustration. Real arbitrage must cover fees and execution costs, so a price gap can remain. If the market price falls instead, the trade direction reverses.

        Arbitrage helps bring pool prices toward market prices. For LPs, those trades can also give up value: the pool sells an asset while its price is catching up.

        Two different comparisons

        Impermanent loss
        Compare the LP’s principal with keeping its starting tokens.
        Loss-versus-rebalancing (LVR)
        Compare with a strategy that makes the same balance changes at external market prices, under its stated assumptions.

        They answer different questions. Adding IL and LVR as two separate charges would mix the benchmarks. This illustration does not estimate LVR or change your results.

        Research: loss-versus-rebalancing ↗

        Competition can change for a single trade

        A provider can add liquidity just before a swap and remove it just after. This is called just-in-time liquidity, or JIT.

        The existing LPs did not withdraw anything. Extra liquidity reduced their share of that trade’s fees. In a real swap it can also improve the trader’s execution, so fee dilution alone does not establish the effect on LP profit.

        A snapshot before or after the trade can miss that competition. Neither this illustration nor the fee batches reconstruct real JIT activity. Uniswap’s JIT research ↗

        How fees are allocated here

        Half of each batch’s gross input value is ETH and half is USDC. The trader fee is taken from each input token. After the chosen protocol share, your active range receives its liquidity divided by the combined liquidity of your range and the other LPs. A and B are alternatives, not positions competing with each other.

        Real pools contain many overlapping ranges that change over time. This experiment groups other LPs into one chosen range; it does not show typical earnings.

        Other LPs start 50/50 by value in one range centered on the starting price, or across the full range. Their entered capital is valued at that starting price. Their width, capital, trading volume, fee tier and protocol share are captured separately for each batch. Changing the shared starting price recenters every range; moving the comparison price does not. Fees remain in tokens without compounding and are valued at the selected comparison price, with USDC = $1.

        The price is held fixed while each batch is allocated. Price impact, routing, tick crossings and fees from moving between batch prices are omitted. Exact range edges require individual swap direction, so batches at an edge are not added. A batch also cannot be added if either alternative has no active LPs. This is an allocation approximation, not a historical replay or executable pool simulation.

        Principal uses the existing continuous range model. Gas, incentives, rebalancing and contract rounding are excluded. The handoff to the range lab converts retained fee tokens into a fixed USD assumption at the selected price.

        What stays equal, and what this model assumes

        Both positions use the same capital, starting ETH price, 50/50 token basket and comparison price. Their lower and upper bounds have the same geometric center. Changing a width changes only those bounds and the resulting liquidity.

        To preserve the starting mix, the upper bound is the starting price multiplied by a factor, and the lower bound is divided by that factor. The distances above and below the starting price are slightly different in dollars. A 20% width is the total span, not ±20%.

        Positions keep their initial liquidity. Fees are zero in the principal results; fee targets are hypothetical USD cash needed to match the common holding basket. The model assumes USDC = $1 and excludes gas, rebalancing, incentives, tick spacing and on-chain rounding.

          How to read this replay

          Range status is a checkpoint fact. An outside sample shows that the position was outside at that block. A return sample is the first recorded in-range checkpoint after an outside sample; it does not identify the exact crossing time.

          Elapsed time and fee income measure different things. The range-time counter measures active seconds. Fee growth measures tokens earned through swaps. They include activity between samples; swaps within one block can earn fees without adding an active second.

          Earned tokens and their dollar value differ. Each interval adds the change in cumulative fee credits. Earlier fee tokens remain held, so their dollar value can move with ETH even while no new fees accrue. Fees are not compounded.

          Holding uses the starting principal tokens. Previously accrued fees and recorded owed balances are excluded from both strategies. Dollar comparisons use each selected pool price and USDC = $1; gas and incentives are excluded. This is a recorded period, not lifetime PnL or a forecast.
