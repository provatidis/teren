01 · HYPOTHETICAL MODELS

What if these assumptions were true?

The full-range lab uses a simplified Uniswap v2-style 50/50 constant-product model to compare a position with holding its starting ETH and USDC. The price-range lab uses simplified Uniswap v3-style concentrated liquidity math to calculate a position for your chosen bounds and compare it with holding its own starting tokens.

The controlled width experiment gives both alternatives the same capital, starting price and 50/50 token basket. It centers both ranges around that price and changes only their width. Principal results exclude fees; the displayed fee target is the cash needed to match the common holding basket. It does not estimate earnings for those ranges.

Uniswap v3 also allows full-range positions. The v2-style and v3-style labels identify the model used in each Teren lab.

Calculation identifiers such as cp50-v2 and cl-range-v2 identify Teren’s model revisions, not the Uniswap protocol version. Earlier revisions remain supported.

Prices, budget and fee income come from your inputs. Fee income is a supplied cash amount or starting capital × assumed average position fee APR × days / 365. The APR covers the whole period, including time out of range; it is not multiplied by concentration. Fees remain separate USD cash after any protocol share, without compounding. They are not estimated from trading activity. Optional opening, collection and closing expenses reduce the result and increase the fee target. These costs are additional to holding and paid separately from starting capital. Some lessons supply fictional fees and costs to help interpret a result; these are labelled teaching facts.

The liquidity labs assume USDC = $1 and ideal price alignment through arbitrage. They calculate endpoints rather than a trading path. They do not estimate gas or earned fees, or model rebalancing, native tick constraints or loss-versus-rebalancing (LVR). Entered costs are assumptions. The holding benchmark keeps the exact starting tokens.

The lending lab uses Aave v3-style collateral concepts with illustrative ETH/USDC inputs and a choice of simple interest or continuous compounding at fixed APRs. The choice applies to supplied ETH, USDC debt, health factor, responses and the liquidation illustration. New experiments default to compounding; older scenarios and existing lessons keep simple interest. It separates the maximum starting loan from the liquidation threshold and keeps borrowed cash beside its matching debt. All rates and collateral rules are editable assumptions, not current market quotes. It assumes USDC = $1 and immediate, cost-free responses; it does not reproduce Aave’s reserve indexes, execute liquidations or reconstruct a price path. A separate single-event illustration shows how debt repayment and a liquidation bonus change the balances at the selected price. Its repayment fraction and bonus are explicit assumptions; it does not reproduce Aave’s complete liquidation rules. A visual time control separates original balances from added interest, using one dollar scale at the chosen ETH price. The optional debt reference compares both rules at the same constant borrow APR. Independently checked lending records are not yet available.

The borrowing-loop lab uses a separate model. Borrowed USDC is spent buying ETH, then supplied as collateral. It tracks each step and compares the result with holding the original ETH. Fixed simple interest or continuous compounding changes the later balances. Its unwind preview withdraws, sells and repays at one fixed price, checking collateral before each sale. It stops at or below the liquidation boundary and retains remaining debt. Swap losses are assumed; gas, changing prices and protocol-specific execution restrictions are excluded.

The virtual swap sandbox is a separate pretend pool. Its trades change its reserves; it does not submit transactions.

CORRELATED-ASSET EXPERIMENT

Staking exposure with ETH debt

The wstETH/WETH experiment separates the conversion rate, market sale price and lender’s oracle price. Staking APY changes stETH per wstETH; an optional additional lending APY changes the supplied wstETH quantity. Each is counted once. WETH borrowing compounds continuously at the chosen APR, with an optional rate change on a selected day. These are assumptions, not current rates.

The original wstETH is supplied before construction. Choose an exact target holding or build in sequential borrowing rounds. A 3× target means three times the original wstETH quantity, not three times the net ETH price exposure. The assumed flash-assisted route buys and supplies the extra tokens, then borrows against the completed collateral to settle the temporary loan and build flash fee. Targets above maximum LTV are rejected. Round mode borrows against each new supply instead. Both include assumed swap losses and exclude build gas.

The holding benchmark retains the original wstETH in a wallet, with the same staking growth. It earns no additional collateral lending yield. A common ETH dollar price affects both sides of the loan and cancels out of its health factor.

A market discount is applied at the selected endpoint. The oracle either follows that discount or tracks the conversion rate; neither choice represents a specific live feed. New setups track conversion value, while saved setups keep their original oracle choice. The rate-only path is also checked for an earlier liquidation boundary. Balances remain before liquidation, and an optional single-event illustration retains unpaid debt. A later healthy endpoint does not undo an earlier boundary.

The atomic exit illustration counts the temporary flash debt: borrow WETH, repay the original debt, release collateral, sell at the market price, then settle the flash loan, fee and flat exit cost. Choose a full sale into WETH or sell only enough to repay and keep the unsold wstETH. Both stop if execution is unavailable, proceeds are insufficient or the position has reached a boundary. Gas must be available up front.

The full-sale comparison sells the original holding with the same swap haircut and flat cost, without a flash fee. Keeping wstETH instead compares the retained tokens with the original tokens held without an exit transaction. Their displayed WETH value is a market valuation; unsold tokens still carry staking exposure and market risk. Older round-based scenarios preserve their original construction, oracle choice and WETH exit.

No transaction is submitted. Market liquidity, caps, actual oracle rules, reserve indexes, flash-loan availability, slashing events and withdrawal queues are not reconstructed. The conversion rate is not an immediate ETH-redemption quote. Aave e-mode categories and Morpho Blue markets need separate adapters and evidence before their current parameters can be displayed as supported settings.

02 · RECORDED SNAPSHOTS

What did this pool or position hold at one block?

Live imports read Ethereum WETH/USDC pools and Uniswap v3 positions at a finalized block. Each result retains its block number, hash, timestamp and data source. The block hash is checked again after the reads.

Principal token amounts and uncollected fees are recomputed from the recorded contract values. A snapshot describes that moment; it does not establish lifetime profit or reconstruct the original deposit.

Exported JSON keeps the accounting inputs for inspection. Importing a file checks its structure, pool identity and accounting. Confirming its claimed contract state independently requires reading Ethereum at the stated block; a valid file or a matching block hash is not sufficient by itself.

03 · HISTORICAL RECORDS · CHECKED AT CAPTURE

What happened to these positions over a recorded period?

The three bundled cases follow real, fixed-range Uniswap v3 positions. The first covers 83 minutes in range; the second covers about 33 hours with a range exit and return. The third follows one week through eight daily checkpoints: no new fee tokens and no recorded active seconds. Its starting and final principal match holding, while its dollar value falls.

Recorded fee-growth counters establish newly earned fee tokens. Range-time counters establish time active between checkpoints. In those three cases, complete event queries check that no deposits, withdrawals or fee collections changed the position during the period.

At capture, read-only collect simulations were compared with the separate fee calculations. These are independent accounting methods, rather than an external audit of the project. State and event providers’ endpoint block hashes and timestamps were checked for agreement. The files retain these checks so the capture can be reproduced with historical Ethereum access. The week joins seven bounded daily records with identical shared checkpoints and checks cumulative fees from the week’s start. The browser validates the bundled evidence and arithmetic; it does not contact the chain again. A manually triggered verification workflow can re-read the original cases and the matched comparison and publish a verification report.

The separate two-range comparison follows narrower and wider positions over the same week, both active at the start. A declared candidate order selects them before their earnings are inspected. Their liquidity stays fixed, but fee collections are allowed: transaction receipts establish actual pool payments, starting collectable balances are subtracted, and the resulting ledger is checked against fee growth. Collection rounding is retained. Each position has its own starting token mix and holding benchmark; this pair cannot isolate the effect of width alone or establish typical earnings.

For this comparison, collected tokens are valued as if held until the selected checkpoint. Later recipient trades are not reconstructed. The export retains the daily and collection-block snapshots, receipts and event coverage.

Holding keeps the exact tokens present at the first checkpoint. Both strategies exclude previously accrued fees. Newly earned fee tokens remain separate and are valued at the selected checkpoint’s pool price. Gas and incentives are excluded, and USDC = $1 is assumed.

The chart connects samples; it does not show every price crossing. These are verified fixed-position records. Changing a range in Experiment starts a new hypothetical example; it does not backtest that range against the record.

Hypothetical fee flow

Follow the fees allocates chosen trading batches at fixed prices. Each range competes separately with the same other LPs. Their capital can occupy one chosen range or the full range. Concentrating that capital changes their active liquidity and your fee share; the visible range is an illustration, not a measured pool distribution. Trader fees are split into an assumed protocol share and LP fees; the active range receives its share by liquidity. Retained ETH and USDC fees remain separate from principal and are valued at the selected comparison price.

This approximation holds price fixed inside each batch and omits price impact and fees from moving between batch prices. It explains fee sharing under supplied assumptions; it does not estimate historical earnings or reconstruct real swaps. Read the model and its limits ↗.

The separate arbitrage and temporary-liquidity illustrations explain mechanisms. They do not estimate LVR, reconstruct JIT activity or alter the experiment. Loss versus holding and loss versus rebalancing use different benchmarks and are not added together.

Read the comparison in context

A result belongs to its prices, starting tokens, period and included costs. Fee income, dollar profit and the gap to holding describe different things. For a loan, a health factor describes liquidation eligibility at the selected price and balances; it does not show whether a real position avoided liquidation earlier. A short recorded outcome does not establish a future yearly return.
